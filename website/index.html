How it works

From “make a video” to
final.mp4

Claude does the parts that need judgment: what to show, how to show it and what to say. The pipeline does the editing.

01What to show

Reads your code

Say what to show in your own words, or point it at a commit. Claude finds the feature in your product's code: the page's route, the labels on its buttons, what shows while something slow runs.

changes.md
02The recording

Drives Chrome itself

Claude clicks through the feature in your product, signed in if it needs to be, while the screen records. If the take doesn't show the change, it records again.

footage/demo.mp4
03The script

Writes what to say

Each scene is written to what's on screen while it's said, and paced to fit. You read it and approve it before anything costs money.

script.jsonYou approve it
04The video

Films, speaks, edits

The intro and outro are filmed from your photo, the narration is spoken in your avatar's voice, and the captions and music are mixed in.

/review-video

Then it tells you why they left.New

Once a video is posted, Claude fetches its numbers from YouTube and Instagram, lines the retention up with the video's own scenes, and looks at the frames where viewers swiped away.

  • Against your own median. Swipe-aways, watch time, likes, shares and subscribers per 1,000 views, next to your other Shorts and Reels from the 90 days before.
  • Where viewers left. How many were still watching at the start and end of the intro, each scene and the outro, and what was said in each.
  • Lessons the next video follows. What worked goes in your product's notes, with its evidence, and /make-video follows it when it writes the next script.

Built for people who ship

It lives in your repo.

A Short for every release, from GitHub Actions

Copy the workflow into your product's repo. With --yes it asks nothing: it runs the skill there with Claude Code on your subscription, using the product notes you committed, and uploads the video.

gh workflow run make-video.yml -f commits=3f2a9c1

Records signed in

Sign in once, by hand, in Chrome. Every recording after starts signed in, in that site's own profile.

English or Hindi, friendly or hype

Scripts in either language, in a calm voice or an excited one, like a Short.

Only remakes what changed

Edit one scene's text and only its narration is spoken again. Edit a file by hand and it's kept as you left it.

Bring your own clips

Filmed your intro in HeyGen, Higgsfield or the Gemini app? Bring it in, a file or a link, and the rest is made around it.

Keys never touch the chat

It opens a file in your editor for the keys your choices need. Paste them there. A Kokoro or silent demo needs none.

FAQ

Good questions.

Anything else is in the README.

What does a video cost?

A narrated demo with Kokoro, or a silent one, costs nothing: Kokoro runs on your machine. An AI avatar video costs about $2 to film with Gemini Omni (about $1 each for the intro and the outro), plus the narration if ElevenLabs speaks it. You approve the script before anything is paid for.

Does it work with my product?

Any web product with an address. If it needs you signed in, you sign in once by hand and recordings start signed in from then on; a demo account is best, since the recording shows it. A site with a phone layout fills the vertical video. One without records at 1280×800 and is shown whole over a blurred copy.

What do I need?

Claude Code and the plugin. The first video takes a few minutes longer while the plugin downloads uv, its Python packages (ffmpeg among them), and Chromium if you don't have Chrome. Run Claude Code in your product's repo, so Claude can read its code.

Who films me, and whose voice is it?

Gemini Omni films a new shot of you from your photo, in the setting the script describes. HeyGen animates your photo as it is. Or film the clips anywhere and bring them in. The narration is spoken in the intro's voice, by ElevenLabs or your own voice API on Modal, so the whole video sounds like one person.

Can it run without me?

Yes. With --yes it asks nothing, so it runs from GitHub Actions in your product's repo, on the commits you name, and uploads the video when it's done.

Does YouTube know it's AI?

Each video comes with a metadata.json that sets YouTube's “altered or synthetic content” flag: on for an avatar or a cloned voice, off for Kokoro's voice or no voice.
