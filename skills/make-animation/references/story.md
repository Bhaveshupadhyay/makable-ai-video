# Turning a fact into a 30-second Short

One question, answered with one surprising number, in 25 to 35 seconds. The shape that holds viewers:

1. **Hook (shot 1): a question the viewer can't answer.** "If a coconut fell 25 metres onto your head, would your
   skull survive?" It's concrete, a little scary and visual, and it names the stakes in the first second. See
   `/make-video`'s [hooks.md](../../make-video/references/hooks.md) for the kinds of hook, and for opening on the
   climax, cut just before it resolves.
2. **The clues, with numbers (shots 2 and 3).** Facts that raise the stakes without answering the question yet: "A
   coconut weighs 1.5 to 2 kg, and from that height it reaches about 80 km/h." One number per sentence, said in
   words the viewer already uses (km/h, not m/s).
3. **The guess (the end of shot 3).** Ask the viewer to answer before you do: "So, would your skull survive?
   Guess in the comments." It gets comments, and gives them a reason to stay for the answer.
4. **The answer (the last shot).** Only now: "A blow like that really can fracture a skull, and doctors have
   recorded such cases." Add the twist or a light, funny release if there is one: "Look up before you nap under
   a palm!" End on it. Don't say "follow": the subscribe animation over the last shot asks for that, if there
   is one. Best of all, the last shot runs back into shot 1, so the video loops.

## Checking the facts

- Search for the key claim before writing anything, and keep the source: a study, an encyclopedia, an agency. Put
  it in the description.
- Work the numbers out yourself when they're physics: v = √(2gh) for a fall (25 m gives about 22 m/s, 80 km/h).
  Say "about" when air or rounding changes them.
- **Leave out myths, however often they're repeated.** "Coconuts kill 150 people a year" has no source: it's from a
  1980s insurance company's press release. A myth in a fact video is the comment that sinks it. If the myth is the
  point, say it's a myth.
- Nothing medical, legal or financial that reads as advice.

## Words

- Hindi: everyday Hindi, as people speak it, with English words where they'd use them (स्पीड, फैक्ट्स). Numbers
  in words, as said (पच्चीस, अस्सी), so the narrator says them right; the captions show them as digits (25, 80).
- About 2.5 words a second, and a short pause after each line. With Omni, each shot's line is said in 4 to 8
  seconds: 10 to 19 words. With H3, in 3 to 6.5 seconds: 7 to 15 words, and 11 or fewer for its best, 5 seconds or
  less. The check warns otherwise.
- A tag or two where the feeling helps (`[surprise-wa]` on the hook, a `[sigh]`): no more than three in the video.
