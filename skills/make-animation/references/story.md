# Turning a fact into a 30-second Short

One question, answered with one surprising number, in 25 to 35 seconds. The shape that holds viewers:

1. **Hook (shot 1): a question the viewer can't answer.** "If a coconut fell 25 metres onto your head, would your
   skull survive?" It's concrete, a little scary and visual, and it names the stakes in the first second. See
   `/make-video`'s [hooks.md](../../make-video/references/hooks.md) for the kinds of hook, and for opening on the
   climax, cut just before it resolves.
2. **The clues, with numbers (shots 2 and 3).** Facts that raise the stakes without answering the question yet: "A
   coconut weighs 1.5 to 2 kg, and from that height it reaches about 80 km/h." One number per sentence, said in
   words the viewer already uses (km/h, not m/s).
3. **The guess (the end of shot 3).** Ask the viewer to answer before you do: "So, would your skull survive?
   Guess in the comments." It gets comments, and gives them a reason to stay for the answer.
4. **The answer (the last shot).** Only now: "A blow like that really can fracture a skull, and doctors have
   recorded such cases." Add the twist or a light, funny release if there is one: "Look up before you nap under
   a palm!" End on it. Don't say "follow": the subscribe animation over the last shot asks for that, if there
   is one. Best of all, the last shot runs back into shot 1, so the video loops.

## A story from the scriptures (the devotional style)

A mythology Short isn't a question and an answer: it's one moment of a story, told so the viewer feels it.
The shape:

1. **Hook (shot 1): the shocking moment, as a question.** "आखिर क्यों [something no one expects of a god] … और
   क्यों/कैसे [something devotees still see today]": "आखिर क्यों शनिदेव की सिर्फ़ एक नज़र पड़ते ही माता पार्वती
   के नन्हे पुत्र का सिर धड़ से अलग हो गया, और क्यों शनिदेव आज भी नज़रें झुकाकर रहते हैं?" Show the moment
   (the glance, the mother's horror), not its answer. Or open on someone's reaction, with a line spoken to the god
   by name. Either way the viewer doesn't know yet who or why, and stays to find out: a climax-first hook
   (`/make-video`'s [hooks.md](../../make-video/references/hooks.md)).
2. **Who and why (shots 2 to 4).** Step back just enough: who this is, what they wanted, what it cost them. A
   child who left the palace, months of tapasya in the forest. One fact of the story a line.
3. **The peak (shots 5 to 7).** The divine moment itself, in light: the god appears, the child's eyes open, the
   beam reaches the sky. Short shots, cut fast.
4. **The meaning (the last shot).** One line of what it means, or the boon given, said simply, tied back to the
   hook's second question: "इसीलिए शनिदेव की नज़रें आज भी झुकी रहती हैं।" Then ask for a comment the devotee
   wants to write: "कमेंट में जय श्री गणेश ज़रूर लिखें।" The last shot can echo the first, so the video loops.

About 60 seconds is the length the user wants for these: 150 to 160 words, 12 to 15 shots with H3.

**Where stories come from.** The scriptures and their traditional retellings: the Bhagavata Purana, Vishnu Purana,
Shiva Purana, Ramayana and Mahabharata, and the Gita Press (Gorakhpur) Hindi editions of them. Good ones are short
and turn on one moment: Dhruva, Prahlada and Narasimha, Markandeya and Yama, Gajendra, Sudama, Hanuman finding
Sanjeevani, Ganesha and the moon, Shiva drinking the poison. Retell them in your own words. Never take another
channel's script, its invented scenes or its pictures: the story is everyone's, their telling is theirs.

**Faithful, not invented.** Check the version you tell (a search, or the text itself) and keep to it: a devotee who
knows the story is the comment that sinks a wrong one. When versions differ, use the best-known one, and name the
text in the description ("Source: Bhagavata Purana, Canto 4"). Don't present a story as history or science, and
don't mock or argue with any belief.

**Words for it.** Reverent, simple Hindi, a little more formal than a fact Short (तपस्या, वरदान, प्रभु), said
slowly: about 2 words a second, so a 5-second shot has 9 or 10 words. A spoken line can be a character's, in the
first person ("मैं तुम्हें वर देता हूँ…"), and the narrator speaks it. Use the `friendly` tone, not `hype`, and
a slower voice (`new --narrator-option speed=0.9`). No `[laughter]` or `[surprise-wa]`: a `[sigh]` at most, where a
character is moved.

## Checking the facts

- Search for the key claim before writing anything, and keep the source: a study, an encyclopedia, an agency. Put
  it in the description.
- Work the numbers out yourself when they're physics: v = √(2gh) for a fall (25 m gives about 22 m/s, 80 km/h).
  Say "about" when air or rounding changes them.
- **Leave out myths, however often they're repeated.** "Coconuts kill 150 people a year" has no source: it's from a
  1980s insurance company's press release. A myth in a fact video is the comment that sinks it. If the myth is the
  point, say it's a myth.
- Nothing medical, legal or financial that reads as advice.

## Words

- Hindi: everyday Hindi, as people speak it, with English words where they'd use them (स्पीड, फैक्ट्स). Numbers
  in words, as said (पच्चीस, अस्सी), so the narrator says them right; the captions show them as digits (25, 80).
- About 2.5 words a second, and a short pause after each line. With Omni, each shot's line is said in 4 to 8
  seconds: 10 to 19 words. With H3, in 3 to 6.5 seconds: 7 to 15 words, and 11 or fewer for its best, 5 seconds or
  less. The check warns otherwise.
- A tag or two where the feeling helps (`[surprise-wa]` on the hook, a `[sigh]`): no more than three in the video.
