# Turning a fact into a 30-second Short

One question, answered with one surprising number, in 25 to 35 seconds. The shape that holds viewers:

1. **Hook (shot 1): a question the viewer can't answer.** "If a coconut fell 25 metres onto your head, would your
   skull survive?" It's concrete, a little scary and visual, and it names the stakes in the first second.
2. **The fact, with a number (shot 2).** "A coconut weighs 1.5 to 2 kg, and from that height it reaches about
   80 km/h." One number per sentence, said in words the viewer already uses (km/h, not m/s).
3. **The comparison or the consequence (shot 3).** What the number means: "a blow like that really can fracture a
   skull, and doctors have recorded such cases."
4. **The twist or the payoff (shot 4).** Something they didn't expect, or a light, funny release.
5. **The sign-off (the last shot, or the end of shot 4).** "Look up before you nap under a palm! Follow for more
   facts like this." Short: it's said over the last shot, not a card.

## Checking the facts

- Search for the key claim before writing anything, and keep the source: a study, an encyclopedia, an agency. Put
  it in the description.
- Work the numbers out yourself when they're physics: v = √(2gh) for a fall (25 m gives about 22 m/s, 80 km/h).
  Say "about" when air or rounding changes them.
- **Leave out myths, however often they're repeated.** "Coconuts kill 150 people a year" has no source: it's from a
  1980s insurance company's press release. A myth in a fact video is the comment that sinks it. If the myth is the
  point, say it's a myth.
- Nothing medical, legal or financial that reads as advice.

## Words

- Hindi: everyday Hindi, as people speak it, with English words where they'd use them (स्पीड, फैक्ट्स). Numbers
  in words, as said (पच्चीस, अस्सी), so the narrator says them right; the captions show them as digits (25, 80).
- About 2.5 words a second. With Omni, each shot's line is said in 4 to 8 seconds: 8 to 18 words. With H3, in 3 to
  6.5 seconds, best at 5 or less: 6 to 14 words. The check warns otherwise.
- A tag or two where the feeling helps (`[surprise-wa]` on the hook, a `[sigh]`): no more than three in the video.
